How Claude runs the Verses Across socials

My brother and I built Verses Across, a free app for reading the world's scriptures side by side. Once it was live, it needed people to actually find it. So I set Claude Code up to run its Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and Facebook Page, and I had a second model check every post before it goes out.

4accounts: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook Page
2×check-ins a day
2models on every post: one writes it, one checks it
1person who OKs anything sensitive: me

How it works

The app and the social media each have their own Claude Code session. When the app session ships something, it sends the social session a quick note: what changed and what's OK to say about it. The social session takes it from there. It figures out what clip to record, writes the caption, and puts it on the schedule for each account.

What it handles

  • The videos. A script records the live app in dark mode and cuts it into vertical clips. For anything that shows reading progress, it'll use a made-up demo profile, not a real person's data.
  • The captions. Everything comes from what actually shipped. Scripture text gets pulled straight from the app's own files, never from memory, and the source and translator are named.
  • The posting. Claude drives the browser: uploads the clip and fills in the caption.
  • The check-ins. Twice a day it goes through the schedule, reads comments, and lets me know about anything that needs me.
  • The setup. It set up the channel and profile pages, plus contact, support, feedback and privacy emails for the app that all land in one inbox, sorted into folders.

Nothing goes up without a second look

One Claude writes the post and a separate Fable model reads it and sends back edits. It's looking for anything the clip doesn't back up, privacy slips, or wording that breaks the rules I set. Anything sensitive waits on me.

Not every catch comes from the reviewer, though. While filming a demo clip, the French column didn't light up like the other languages. The caption got changed so it didn't say "every language," and the app session fixed the French that same morning.

The rules are written down

  • Older translations get labelled with their edition and translator. AI translations get marked as AI.
  • Don't announce a feature before it's live.
  • One schedule file runs everything, and finished items get checked off with the date.
  • Never post the same thing twice.

I decide what the accounts should be and what the rules are, I OK the stuff that needs a person, and when something slips, I figure out why and change the process so it doesn't happen again. Claude does the building and the day-to-day.
